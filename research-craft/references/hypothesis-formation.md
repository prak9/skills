# From Intuition To Discriminating Hypotheses

Use when the task needs an initial explanation of an unfamiliar mechanism, an ambiguous concept carries the conclusion, or existing observations fit materially different explanations. These are optional investigative lenses, not a fixed thinking sequence or a required number of hypotheses.

## Generate Plausible Explanations

Start from the outcome, population, horizon and decision already in scope. Look for constraints the system cannot evade; trace intermediate mechanisms, actor choices, costs and feedback. A task's hard constraint is different from an assumed business model or a person's preference.

Draw candidates from constraints, mechanism knowledge, counterexamples, structurally similar problems and systematic residuals. Use whichever source is informative; no universal ordering applies. For an analogy, name the shared causal structure and the boundary that may prevent transfer. Draw feedback through time rather than treating a circular diagram as proof of causation.

Include the strongest plausible alternative when it could change the decision: another mechanism, a common cause, selection/measurement effects, reverse direction, or a stage-dependent explanation. Do not pad the answer with artificial opponents. More data or a fluent causal story does not automatically identify which mechanism generated the observation.

## Make The Difference Observable

Keep a compact comparison in the existing research record:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Claim and boundary | What exactly is asserted, for whom and when? |
| Mechanism | Through which intermediate states or decisions would it act? |
| Rival | What else could produce the observation? Can both explanations hold? |
| Differential prediction | In what accessible circumstance do they predict different outcomes? |
| Measurement | What unit, denominator, population, source and observation time would distinguish them? |
| Update | Which result would favor, weaken or leave each explanation unresolved? |

Choose the cheapest feasible observation that can change the judgment. If both explanations predict the same available evidence, report them as observationally unresolved; do not award the result to the better story. A discriminating association can inform a hypothesis without proving a causal effect; causal claims still need an identification argument and its limits.

Example: falling unit cost after vertical integration is compatible with integration synergy and an industry-wide component-price decline. Compare appropriately matched cost components, timing and external benchmarks before assigning credit. If comparable evidence is unavailable, bound the claim instead of inventing a test result.

## Operationalize Without Replacing The Concept

For a load-bearing label such as autonomy, moat or data flywheel, select measurable implications. Specify the denominator, task difficulty, inclusion rules, failure reporting and what the proxy cannot establish. Ask for a plausible case where the metric improves while the claimed capability does not.

For example, fewer human interventions can reflect better autonomy or removal of difficult tasks. Compare intervention frequency, duration/cost and task completion under a comparable task distribution; do not infer a capability multiplier from the proxy alone. Do not add every conceivable metric—inspect the ambiguity that changes this decision.

Check whether contrary outcomes can always be reinterpreted to save the label. If so, narrow the claim and specify an observation that would lower confidence. A useful metaphor need not be a valid predictive model.

## Resolve Claims Without Rewriting History

For long-horizon claims, identify nearer observations only where the mechanism supports them. Distinguish necessary conditions, supportive indicators and alternative paths. A missing supportive signal or unpublished metric is not automatically a failed necessary condition. Record maturation, disclosure lag and review timing; do not fabricate a year-by-year roadmap.

Use probabilities only for sufficiently defined, resolvable events and disclose their basis. Mechanisms can coexist: do not force their probabilities to sum to one unless the alternatives are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Do not multiply dependent milestones as if independent. Unsupported point probabilities may remain ranges or qualitative judgments.

When a forecast journal is requested or already authorized, reuse it. Give consequential claims stable IDs and retain the original information set, prediction, settlement rule/date/source, dependencies, observation and reason for any update. Resolve event accuracy, mechanism support and decision payoff separately. A winner does not identify its cause; unresolved is a legitimate result. Version changed definitions or alternative paths instead of retroactively declaring the original prediction successful.

One miss can be compatible with a probabilistic forecast. Examine predeclared tolerances, comparable resolved cases and structured errors before changing the model. Before interpreting residuals as a new mechanism, check data identity, labels, leakage, selection, implementation and chance variation. Use residuals to propose a new test on fresh evidence, not to fit an exception to the same failure.

This adapter neither creates a new ledger nor authorizes monitoring, contact, trading or other external actions. A supported negative or unresolved finding can complete the requested research.
