# Evaluate Human Learning

Use only when evaluating instruction, training or a tool's effect on a learner. Distinguish assisted output quality, independent ability, retention and transfer; a production workflow can succeed without teaching its user.

Match evidence to the claimed result:

- Assisted product scores measure assisted performance, not unaided competence.
- Independent ability needs unseen, comparably difficult tasks without the assistance under test.
- Retention needs a relevant later interval that is actually observed, not merely scheduled.
- Transfer needs a changed context that preserves the target mechanism.

Test only the dimensions claimed. Preserve prerequisite knowledge, baseline ability, allowed aids, exposure time and scoring conditions. Different practice or assistance limits before/after attribution; causal claims need an appropriate comparison and identification argument, not merely a higher post-test score.

Separate learner-generated explanations, predictions and corrections from model-supplied work. Satisfaction, confidence and an AI tutor's approval do not substitute for demonstrated application. An unattempted check is missing evidence, not a pass. One exercise supports a narrow observation, not durable mastery or broad cognitive development.

Reuse the existing evaluation record. A test design does not authorize recruitment, scheduled follow-up or new external data collection. Do not impose learning tests on a request whose only claim is delivery quality or efficiency.
