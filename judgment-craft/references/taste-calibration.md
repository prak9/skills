# Taste Calibration

Use this adapter to train the judgment of **which problem deserves scarce attention**. Taste is not eloquence, trend-following, or polished execution. It is the ability to notice an important, tractable, and underappreciated opportunity before the answer is obvious.

## Separate Selection From Execution

Ask three different questions:

1. **Selection:** Was this worth attempting relative to available alternatives?
2. **Execution:** Given the choice, was the work done well?
3. **Outcome:** What happened, including luck and external change?

A beautifully executed trivial problem is still a weak selection. A strong selection can fail through poor execution or variance. Never use one dimension to excuse or condemn another.

## Keep An Opportunity Forecast Portfolio

At a fixed cadence, record more plausible opportunities than you can pursue. Do this before choosing so rejected ideas remain available for calibration.

```text
Recorded date / resolution date:
Problem or opportunity:
Who benefits and why it may matter:
Why now; what changed:
Decisive crux and assumptions:
Why it may be tractable:
Why it may be neglected or mispriced:
Observable forecast by the resolution date:
Resolution source:
Chosen? yes / no; reason and opportunity cost:
Smallest evidence-buying bet, if chosen:
```

Use a portfolio, not one prediction. Include distinct domains or approaches when breadth could reveal a better problem class.

## Resolve Without Hindsight

At the declared horizon, backfill every entry, including ideas not pursued:

- Did the problem become important in the observable way predicted?
- Did someone else solve it or reveal a valuable approach? What signal did you miss?
- Is it still open because it remains neglected, because it is intractable, or because it never mattered?
- Was the original crux right? Which premise broke?
- How good was selection, independent of your execution and outcome luck?
- Which discarded idea would have dominated the chosen bet, given information available at the time?

Preserve misses and false positives. Update a selection heuristic only after repeated evidence, not to rationalize the latest outcome.

## Use AI Without Outsourcing Taste

Use AI to widen the candidate set, retrieve precedents, sharpen forecasts, and schedule resolution. The responsible person still owns values, attention allocation, and the final choice. When importance has no single measurable objective, preserve the competing values instead of inventing a fake score.

Use `research-craft` to make forecasts resolvable and gather evidence. Use `define-problem` after an opportunity is selected to turn it into a precise problem contract.
