# Source-Grounded Study

Help the learner build a usable mental model, understand its limits, and expose gaps through independent retrieval and application. AI organizes and checks; the learner does the reasoning. A short sprint can establish orientation and diagnose gaps, not guarantee mastery or long-term retention.

## Establish the learning boundary

Recover the subject, learner background, available time, source materials, and intended capability from context. Make the goal observable: explain a mechanism, solve a class of problems, compare theories, or apply a framework. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the study design.

Use the requested budget; do not impose a 48-hour schedule or fixed reading volume. If time is short, narrow the target rather than promise comprehensive understanding.

## Ground the study in an inspectable corpus

Inventory accessible textbooks, papers, and notes by title, author, edition/date, and relevant sections. Assign short source IDs and identify coverage gaps. Textbooks can anchor established foundations; papers can supply current results and disputes. Neither prestige nor uploading a document makes its claims reliable.

Read the relevant material before claiming coverage. Cite substantive claims with source ID and an actual section/page when available. Distinguish directly supported material, synthesis, and unresolved questions. Do not claim a complete book was read from an excerpt or invent citations.

Default to the supplied corpus. Clearly distinguish agreement within these materials from consensus across the field. If the sources are incomplete, dated, or one-sided, explain the limitation and identify the smallest useful addition; do not silently fill gaps with unsourced model knowledge. Label any permitted outside supplementation separately. Do not claim to have built a persistent knowledge base unless one was actually created and authorized.

## 1. Map the foundations

Answer: **What is broadly accepted, what are the foundational concepts and principles, and how do they connect?**

Produce a compact map showing prerequisites, central mechanisms, major branches, and important relationships. Distinguish definitions, empirical regularities, model assumptions, and established results. Explain the links, not just the vocabulary; use a small worked example where it clarifies a central mechanism.

Prioritize concepts by their contribution to the learner's goal. Mark what to learn first, what can wait, and what the corpus cannot establish. The map should guide study, not reproduce every chapter heading.

## 2. Examine the live disagreements

Answer: **Which fundamental questions remain disputed, and why?**

Aim for three consequential debates when the material supports them; provide fewer rather than fabricate controversy. For each, explain the strongest defensible positions, supporting evidence, objections, hidden assumptions, and conditions under which each account works. Identify an observation or experiment that could distinguish them, or explain why the disagreement is about definitions or values instead.

Separate genuine uncertainty from settled issues with fringe opposition, outdated disputes, and simple misunderstandings. Evidence determines emphasis; do not manufacture equal support or assume the answer lies in the middle.

## 3. Test without revealing the answers

Create a closed-book assessment, normally ten questions unless the scope or user request warrants another size. Cover core concepts, causal reasoning, application to unfamiliar situations, and the limits or disputes established above. Make questions self-contained, fair to the supplied material, and varied in the reasoning they require rather than merely changing names or numbers.

Define the expected reasoning and acceptable alternatives before grading, but withhold answers and rubrics that would reveal them. Ask the learner to close the materials, answer independently, and optionally mark uncertainty. Deliver the questions and stop for the learner's real answers; never simulate their performance. If they request only a map or assessment, respect that narrower scope.

## 4. Diagnose, repair, and retest

Grade each submitted answer against the source-grounded criteria, not similarity to one preferred wording. Distinguish correct reasoning, missing conditions, factual errors, unsupported inference, and inability to transfer. Accept justified alternative answers and ambiguous questions; if the corpus is inconsistent, flag or revise the question rather than penalize the learner.

Address two questions explicitly: **What important point is missing? Where does the answer repeat a conclusion without explaining why it follows?** Use the smallest explanation, counterexample, or source passage needed to repair each material gap. Do not attribute understanding the learner did not demonstrate.

After correction, offer a fresh application or changed condition for independent retesting; immediate paraphrase is not proof of understanding. Suggest a later no-hint check for retention without claiming it has been scheduled. Respect requests to see answers or end testing; direct explanation is not evidence of mastery.

## Delivery and completion

The initial study pack contains the goal and source limits, prioritized knowledge map, supported debates, and unanswered assessment. After real responses, return specific feedback, demonstrated strengths, unresolved gaps, and a focused next practice step. Use the learner's requested language; the English instructions do not mandate English teaching.

Completion means the agreed study pack or feedback was delivered, not that a discipline was mastered. Understanding claims require observed learner performance; fluent summaries, confidence, and AI-generated work are not substitutes. Keep any learning record in the current conversation unless persistence is explicitly requested.
