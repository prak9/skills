# Mode D — Serenity Alpha (News To Financial Statements)

Use when the user supplies news, a product launch, technology change, procurement clue, earnings-call signal, or asks for small-cap beneficiaries and financial-statement transmission.

Start with this chain:

```text
news -> observed signal -> latent demand hypothesis -> rival explanations
-> independent corroboration -> revenue/profit transmission
-> company-scale elasticity -> validation path
```

## Workflow

1. **Separate talk from demand.** Prefer paid adoption, deployments, shipments, backlog, price, lead time, production schedules, filings, and unprompted management commentary. If demand is not observable, classify the idea as watchlist-only. Cluster orders, shortages, price rises, and commentary caused by one event; they are not independent confirmations.
2. **Translate into financial lines.** Map units, ASP, mix, customer count, usage, take rate, backlog conversion, capacity, input cost, utilization, opex, working capital, capex, and reporting lag. Check competition, substitution, qualification, delivery, financing, currency, M&A, and accounting alternatives.
3. **Rank business purity and elasticity.** Prefer a company only when the demand shock is large relative to its revenue/profit base, exposure is direct, supply position is defensible, attention is limited, and validation can reach reported results within 1–4 quarters.
4. **Test market misclassification.** State the old market label, proposed new role, what price already reflects, and the numbers required for relabeling. A story without reported validation is not alpha.
5. **Build the evidence chain.** Name confirming, weakening, and falsifying observations across company, customer, supplier, and competitor sources.
6. **Score only when useful.** Qualitatively or 1–5 compare demand certainty, transmission clarity, purity, company-scale elasticity, neglect, validation speed, and downside. Do not average away a broken transmission link.
7. **Size by evidence.** Use conditional postures: observe; very small test; add after financial proof; trade-only when consensus/valuation catches up; reject when falsified.

## Output

```markdown
## Conclusion: Primary Company To Verify
## Surface News And Already-Observed Demand
## Financial-Statement Transmission
## First-, Second-, And Third-Order Beneficiaries
## Pure / Elastic Candidates And Current Scale
## Market Misclassification And What Price Embeds
## 1–4 Quarter Verification Chain
## Downside, Transmission Breaks, And Falsifier
## Conditional Exposure Posture
## Sources And Unverified Inputs
```

Open and close with the primary candidate and the single evidence threshold that confirms or kills the thesis. If no candidate is investable, say that first rather than forcing a name.
