# Narrative, Scarcity And Commercialization — Development Cases

Hypothetical visible cases, not executed model results. Give the executor the request and applicable skill/reference, withholding acceptance criteria. Preserve actual outputs and model/harness/instruction versions before grading. Judge reasoning, numerical bridges and scope rather than keywords. These development cases need fresh counterparts for independent confirmation.

| Request | Observable acceptance criteria |
|---|---|
| A company's viral launch doubles its share price. Paid usage, signed contracts and hiring are unchanged. Has its business been validated? | Separates investor attention/pricing from operating validation; identifies a concrete commercial observation rather than inferring demand or automatically calling the company fraudulent. |
| Follow-up: a completed equity financing at the higher price now funds a previously unfunded plant. Construction has not begun. What changed? | Recognizes an actual funding improvement and the price-to-financing feedback; preserves construction/demand risk and evaluates share issuance alongside funded value rather than treating all narrative effects as empty hype. |
| A verified prototype works. The founder has strong laboratory experience but no manufacturing history; permits and scale-up financing remain unresolved. Forecast the next research priority. | Credits technical progress, shifts attention to material next-stage constraints and relevant team capabilities, and avoids prescribing a dip-then-rally stock path. |
| A company is the only listed pure-play accessible to a specified fund mandate. Two comparable eligible rivals will list next month. Its customer retention remains strong. Does its moat disappear? | Distinguishes listed-exposure scarcity from operating advantage; examines possible redistribution of investor demand without claiming customer economics deteriorated or predicting a certain price fall. |
| A thinly traded company has 10 million outstanding shares. Next month 3 million already-issued locked shares become tradable; separately, 2 million warrants could be exercised for cash. Does supply stay fixed? | Separates unlock-related float change from warrant-related share issuance and proceeds; treats exercise and holder sales as conditional. Neither adds locked shares again to outstanding shares nor equates eligibility to sell with actual selling. |
| Hypothetical, same valuation date and risk basis: standalone equity value is 100 with 10 shares. Issue 2 shares for 20 total, immediately spend the 20 on a project whose net future cash flows have present value 30. Ignore fees and all other changes. Is dilution necessarily harmful? | Computes post-investment equity value 130 and per-share value about 10.83 versus 10; old holders retain 10/12 worth about 108.33. Does not add the spent cash again or confuse lower ownership percentage with lower per-share value. Treats project value as a supplied premise. |
| A business issued no equity, but financed expansion with short-term debt and overdue suppliers while postponing maintenance. Is growth self-funded and sustainable? | Reconciles cash sources, maturity, payables and maintenance needs; treats no issuance as financing information, not proof of durable internal funding. Avoids declaring inevitable failure without the amounts/terms. |
| A drilling-services subsidiary funds exploration. Both depend on commodity prices, and the lender's facility shrinks when collateral values fall. Stress the claim that internal funding protects exploration during a downturn. | Traces the joint shock to service cash, exploration economics and borrowing capacity; examines liquidity timing and feasible adjustments rather than assuming independent funding cushions. |
| A disaster contractor's current profit requires two major storms hitting dense cities. Next year's forecast predicts above-average storm count, but landfall locations are unknown. Can we carry current profit forward? | Separates storm count from location, intensity and payable repair demand; bounds profit under relevant demand scenarios and financing commitments, without inventing landfall odds or using an exceptional year as recurring earnings. |
| A profitable microcap has no institutional holders, and the CEO calls it bear-market-proof. | Limits the observation to existing institutional sell inventory; considers other holders, liquidity and business/funding exposure. Neither promises future institutional buying nor assumes low ownership is intrinsically bad. |
| Compute revenue growth from 50 to 60 on a comparable basis; no stock thesis requested. | Gives 20% directly, without a scarcity analysis, commercialization map or new scenario table. |
