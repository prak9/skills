# Resilience, Expansion And Evidence Reliability — Development Cases

Visible hypothetical cases, not executed model results. Give the executor the request and applicable skill/reference without the acceptance criteria. Preserve actual responses and model/harness/instruction versions before grading. Evaluate economic reasoning, affected model dependencies and task scope rather than phrase matching.

| Request | Observable acceptance criteria |
|---|---|
| A century-old brand still serves customers, but its former company entered restructuring and original common shares were cancelled. Does its age prove shareholder-friendly resilience? | Distinguishes business continuity from organization and shareholder outcomes; examines adaptation, rescue terms and capital cost rather than assigning a longevity premium or rejecting the current business automatically. |
| A long-lived business was acquired at a premium, paying its shareholders in cash and ending its independent listing. Does loss of independence mean failed survival? | Separates acquisition from collapse and evaluates shareholder proceeds against the relevant investment basis; neither labels all exits failures nor assumes the premium alone establishes a good lifetime return. |
| A niche supplier's largest customer requests a custom product for another country. There is no signed order; local servicing and certification costs are unknown. Management says customers are pulling it into a huge market. | Treats the request as a demand lead, investigates paid commitment, reusable capabilities and incremental costs, and preserves concentration risk. Does not extrapolate core margins or declare the expansion worthless solely for being early. |
| Follow-up: several unrelated customers now place paid repeat orders for the same new product. Segment evidence shows positive contribution after local delivery/support costs, but expansion capex remains unknown. | Strengthens the repeatable-demand and unit-economics conclusions while leaving full investment returns conditional on capital needs; neither freezes all judgment nor assumes core ROIC transfers. |
| A reported cash balance of 100 supports a net-cash valuation after debt of 40. An original auditor notice says confirmation of 80 of that cash was unavailable. Assess the valuation; the business operations are otherwise unchanged. | Preserves the reported balance and uncertainty, identifies net-cash/liquidity/value dependencies, and stops presenting 60 as verified net cash. Does not declare the disputed 80 fictitious, automatically treat the remaining 20 as confirmed, or invent a recovery probability. Uses supported evidence or explicit conditional scenarios and identifies the confirmation needed. |
| A company changed auditors through a documented routine tender; no disagreement or affected account is disclosed. Should we apply the same disputed-cash adjustment? | Reads the supplied scope and avoids an automatic fraud conclusion or arbitrary haircut; distinguishes ordinary appointment changes from a substantiated account-reliability dispute. |
| A company replaced its auditor after a cash-confirmation dispute. The new auditor has not yet issued findings; management repeats the old balance. Is the net-cash thesis restored? | Keeps the affected input unresolved pending evidence addressing the specific dispute, while leaving unrelated supported analysis intact. Does not treat the new appointment or repeated assertion as confirmation. |
| Extract the reported cash of 100 from the supplied statement only; no reliability dispute or quality analysis is requested. | Gives the reported amount with its period/basis, without forcing a survival study, adjacency review or personal financial questionnaire. |
